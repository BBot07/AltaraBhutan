Six Days In Bumthang:
A Valley Diary

6 June 2026

Bumthang is the part of Bhutan that people speak about softly. Four connected valleys at around 2,600 to 3,000 meters, scattered with ancient temples, farmhouses, apple orchards and pine forest, it feels unhurried in a way even Bhutan's other valleys do not. Six days is a generous amount of time to give it. Here is how a slow week there tends to unfold.

  1. 01

    Days One And Two: Arriving, And The Chokhor Valley

    Most journeys reach Bumthang by crossing the Yotong La, a high pass whose forest of rhododendron and prayer flags is a good first lesson in the region. After a settling-in afternoon, the Chokhor valley is the place to begin: the white walls of Jakar Dzong above town, the seventh-century Jambay Lhakhang, and the temple of Kurje, where Guru Rinpoche is said to have left the imprint of his body in a rock.

  2. 02

    Day Three: Tamshing And A Slow Walk

    Tamshing Lhakhang, founded in the early 1500s by the treasure-revealer Pema Lingpa, holds some of the oldest murals in the country and a heavy chain-mail coat that visitors can carry around the temple for merit. Afterward, walk the footpaths between farmhouses; you will pass buckwheat fields, apple orchards and stone water-mills, with a monastery on every ridge.

  3. 03

    Day Four: The Tang Valley And The Burning Lake

    The Tang valley is wilder and quieter. At Mebar Tsho, the Burning Lake, a gorge pool beneath a cliff face, Pema Lingpa is said to have retrieved hidden treasures and left a lasting blessing. Offer a small butter lamp, take a cup of tea, and stay as long as you like. Tang's farmhouses and small guesthouses are good places to meet local families.

  4. 04

    Day Five: Weavers And The Table

    Chume, known for its yathra woollen weaving, is a morning for watching loom work and, if you like, trying a few passes yourself. Bumthang has its own food traditions: buckwheat pancakes called puta and noodles, local cheese, honey and apple products. Our guides know the family kitchens worth stopping at, and we are happy to arrange a home-cooked lunch.

  5. 05

    Day Six: Departure Without Hurry

    Leave time for one last quiet moment: early prayers at a temple, a final walk, or simply a long breakfast looking at the valley. Whether you fly out from Bathpalathang or drive on toward Trongsa and the west, the road itself is part of the journey, and we plan it with plenty of room to stop.

Bumthang rewards the traveler who stays a little longer than planned. Our Bumthang Spiritual Trail follows much of this route, and we can stretch it, shorten it or combine it with the east of the country to suit your pace.
