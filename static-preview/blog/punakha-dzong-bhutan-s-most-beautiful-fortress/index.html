Punakha Dzong:
Bhutan's Most Beautiful Fortress

2 April 2026

Ask a Bhutanese guide to name the country's most beautiful building and many will answer without hesitation: Punakha Dzong. It sits on a spit of land where two rivers meet, its whitewashed walls and gilded roofs doubled in the water below. It is also a working monastery and seat of government, which is why the best visits are slow ones. Here is what to know before you walk across the bridge.

  1. 01

    A Fortress Built On A Promise

    Punakha Dzong was completed in the late 1630s under Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal, the lama who first unified Bhutan. It served as the seat of government until the capital moved to Thimphu in the 1950s, and it remains the winter home of the central monastic body and its Je Khenpo, the chief abbot. Fire, flood and earthquake have damaged it more than once; each time it was restored, which is part of why the building feels so alive rather than preserved.

  2. 02

    Where Two Rivers Meet

    The dzong stands at the confluence of the Pho Chhu and the Mo Chhu, the “father” and “mother” rivers. Approach on foot across the long wooden cantilever bridge and the whole fortress opens up slowly across the water. Stand on the far bank at the start and end of the day when the light is soft and the colour of the river changes from milky turquoise to deep green.

  3. 03

    Courtyards, Temples And The Utse

    Inside, three courtyards step up toward the utse, the central tower. The first courtyard holds the administrative offices, the second the monastic quarters, and the third the great assembly hall with its enormous carved pillars and painted murals. Your guide will explain the iconography as you go — the wheel of life, the protector deities, the stories from the life of the Buddha. Some rooms are open only at certain hours, so a local guide makes a real difference.

  4. 04

    When To Go

    March to May and October to November are the clearest seasons. In late spring the jacaranda trees in the courtyard flower a soft violet against the white walls, a sight that has become one of Punakha's signatures. If your dates allow, the Punakha Drubchen and Tshechu in late winter bring masked dances and processions to the courtyards. Dates follow the lunar calendar and shift each year, so check before you plan.

  5. 05

    Visiting Well

    • Dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered, and a jacket for the cool courtyards
    • Remove shoes and hats before entering any temple room
    • Photography is welcome in the courtyards but not inside the shrine rooms
    • Allow at least two hours, and add time for the walk across the bridge and along the river
    • Visit with a licensed guide: access to certain halls depends on the day's rituals

Punakha is easy to pair with Dochula Pass, the Chimi Lhakhang fertility temple and a gentle walk through the rice terraces at Sopsokha. Our Royal Capital Immersion journey builds the dzong into a slow, private day, and we are always happy to tailor the route around your dates.
